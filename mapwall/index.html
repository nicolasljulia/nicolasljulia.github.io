The strait went quiet in March 2026: ports inside the Gulf lost two-thirds of their traffic, ports outside it a third

Drag the two bands under the chart to set a baseline period and a comparison period. Each port's outline shows its baseline traffic; the filled disc shows the comparison. Switch vessel type, and click any port to trace it.

Source: IMF PortWatch (UN Global Platform AIS), daily port calls for 46 ports with ≥ 30 calls since July 2025, and daily Strait of Hormuz transit calls. Ports east of 56.3°E and south of 26°N are counted as outside the strait. Coastline: Natural Earth 1:10m.

Famine moves across Sudan, one locality at a time

Two IPC periods sit on top of each other. Drag the black handle to wipe from one to the other, choose what the colours measure, and click localities (or pick a region) to add up how many people are in each phase in both periods.

⇆

Source: IPC Acute Food Insecurity analysis for Sudan via HDX (area-level populations by phase; current Feb–May 2026 and two projections). What is within walking distance in the world's largest refugee settlement?

Click anywhere inside the camps to drop a shelter pin, then set how far someone can walk. The panel lists what is in reach and how far the nearest of each service is. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Toggle the 2021 fire footprint and landslide-prone facilities.

Source: ISCG / IOM Site Management, camp outlines (April 2023) and Common Facility Mapping v26.1; UNOSAT/REACH fire extent of 22 March 2021, via HDX. Distances are straight-line, not along paths; terrain in the camps is steep, so real walking distances are longer.

How much of Funafuti stays dry?

Raise the sea and see which land floods. Switch between a bathtub fill and flooding that has to connect to the ocean or lagoon, add a king tide on top, and drag a line across any islet to draw its elevation profile.

The reservoir that became a forest

Two sliders. The horizontal one moves through time; the vertical one wipes between the satellite image (above the line) and a land-cover map computed from it (below). Click anywhere to see how that spot changed across all eleven dates.

SATMAP

Imagery: Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A via Earth Search (AWS), cloud-masked best-pixel mosaics per period. Land cover is classified on each mosaic: water (MNDWI > 0 with low NIR), bare (NDVI < 0.15), sparse vegetation (0.15–0.35) and dense vegetation (> 0.35). The reservoir footprint is the water mapped in July 2022. The Kakhovka dam was destroyed on 6 June 2023.

Satellite says burned. Inspectors say destroyed.

18,428 structures inspected on the ground, laid over Sentinel-2 imagery before, just after and in the months following the fire. Change the image date, fade in burn severity, and drag across the histogram to select a burn-severity range and see which buildings fall in it. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan.

Structures: CAL FIRE Damage Inspection (DINS) 2025 Eaton, public view. Fire perimeter: Palisades and Eaton dissolved perimeters (21 January 2025). Burn severity: differenced Normalized Burn Ratio (dNBR, Sentinel-2 B8A/B12) against the 18 December 2024 image, with USGS class thresholds (0.1, 0.27, 0.44, 0.66).

The year the Rio Negro ran out of river

Nine cloud-masked images of the Rio Negro and Solimões at Manaus, 2022–2026, from July high water to October low water. Move through time and pin a reference date to trace its shoreline in gold. Drag a line across the river to measure its wet width on every date.

Imagery: Sentinel-2 L2A, 30 m, cloud-masked mosaics per month. Water: MNDWI > 0 with low NIR (sediment-laden Solimões water included). Areas are of the mapped window only and count clear pixels; widths are measured along your line on each image.

Mapping the clearing, year by year

Every polygon of forest that Brazil's PRODES programme mapped as cleared, coloured by the year it fell. Drag the year, move the circular scanner to see the real Sentinel-2 image underneath, and brush the bar chart to isolate a range of years. Click a polygon for its record.
